# Two older BSL approximation trials established no useful native-relative gain

The fixed approximate POM cache and whole-ray reflected-cloud shortcut did not establish a useful benefit at their tested PBR wall. Both trials kept the native original and return control; their drift prevents a precise causal slowdown estimate. A separate projected-UV POM cue rendered without a visible marker and admitted neither an ordinary quality run nor a cost comparison.

| Trial | Original FPS | Full-work control FPS | Candidate FPS | Original-return FPS |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Approximate POM cache | 95 / 93 | 95 / 91 | 80 / 83 | 86 / 87 |
| Whole-ray reflected-cloud shortcut | 96 / 92 | 85 / 79 | 84 / 87 | 88 / 90 |

## Conditions and limitations

Evidence level: source-inspected historical import. Each cell contains two cached instantaneous `Minecraft.getFps()` readbacks at comparison ticks 600/1800, not continuous throughput, a GPU cost or independent repetitions. M3 Max/macOS, OpenGL 4.1 Metal 91.7, Minecraft 1.21.1/NeoForge 21.1.253, Iris 1.8.12/Sodium 0.6.13, BSL 10.1.8, native 1920×1200, intended PBR wall, no DH. Source and configuration checks, quality limits, previous failed attempts and original-return drift remain in the reports.

No new measurements or image review were performed. The reports' image observations are retained as historical observations; raw images are not required by a new visual-equivalence claim because none is made. An empty cue is not proof of zero eligible fragments or a failed mathematical policy. These trials do not reject POM or reflected-cloud optimization generally.

## Decision

Discard those fixed implementations at their tested scope; retain the mechanism, baseline and cue-admission lessons. Do not resurrect the same profile as a proven gain or treat control-relative performance as native-relative performance. A materially changed mechanism needs fresh workload and quality evidence.
